The art of selling gemstone jewellery

Gemmology professor Nona Dronova has worked with precious stones for forty years: at the jeweller’s bench, in the laboratory and in creating jewellery brands. In this course she teaches the key skill of a sales consultant — talking about a stone or metal so that the customer sees its value and wants to wear the piece.

How to talk about design

  • “Our design is similar to…” — BVLGARI, Cartier, Tiffany & Co.
  • “The design is inspired by…” — a softer, more precise wording

Professor’s note. Comparing a piece with world-famous houses helps the customer grasp its style quickly. Say it orally during a consultation, and honestly: “inspired by” is more accurate than “similar to”. Do not use other companies’ trademarks in advertising, on the website or on tags: advertising law prohibits incorrect comparison and use of other brands.

  7. 7Alive

Phrases for selling emerald

  • A timeless value
  • A good investment
  • A stone that brings good luck
  • A symbol of wealth
  • Exquisite and unique
  • A true work of art and nature
  • A stone that never goes out of fashion

How to use the lessons’ words

  • One or two words, not all at once. Customers remember an image, not a list. Pick what is visible in this particular piece: colour, clarity, the sheen of the metal.
  • Word plus proof. “See how alive the colour is in daylight” is stronger than just “alive”. Show the stone by the window, through a loupe, on the customer’s hand.
  • The sales phrase comes last. “A timeless value” or “a good investment” is convincing once the customer has fallen for the piece, not in the first minute.
  • Only the truth about the stone. “Natural” and “genuine” describe natural stones only. From 1 September 2026 these words may not be used for synthetic stones in Russia (see chapter 11). Honesty in description is the basis of trust and repeat purchases.

Practical task

Take three pieces from your display: one in platinum and two with stones. For each, choose two words from the lessons and one sales phrase that truly fit it. Say them aloud to a colleague while showing the piece, and ask: would you believe it as a customer?
