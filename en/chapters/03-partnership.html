Building partnerships

To stay independent even while forming partnerships, you must always be necessary and needed. The more others rely on you, the more freedom you have. Make the prosperity of your alliance depend on you, and you have nothing to fear. Never teach your partners everything you know and can do, so that they cannot manage on their own. If you have to ask a partner for help, do not bother reminding them of your past good deeds. Instead, in your request or your agreement, let them see something that will benefit them, and stress it without restraint. Then they will support the proposal with enthusiasm.

Find out what benefits a partnership can bring your company.

You should never start any collaboration without a precise statement of both parties’ goals. A partnership can bring many positive results: access to new resources, additional services, joint marketing opportunities, a wider network and distribution.

Business alliances are formed according to a particular strategy and with specific expected results. Even so, the parties must accept from the outset that twists and turns on the business road are inevitable. And if an unexpected but profitable turn appears, both sides must be flexible and take it. Otherwise someone else will take it for them.

Partnership is nothing new in the business world, but now it has also become an important means of achieving success. It is an indisputable fact that neither large nor small companies survive alone.

To be the best, you need to make agreements with the best. Find the businesses that produce the best goods and services and can most improve your own.

Business relationships are much like marriage. Success often depends on both partners’ ability to shift their mindset from “I” to “we”.

There are general criteria for reaching agreement:

  • A stable position for both sides. Each partner must be strong and able to bring something of value to the joint venture. The motivation should be aimed at future prospects, not at compensating for past weaknesses.
  • Necessity. For both partners the relationship must rest on strategic goals. A business alliance always serves long-term plans.
  • Interdependence. The partners must need each other and have non-competing capabilities and assets which, combined, achieve results neither could achieve alone.
  • Mutual investment. The partners should invest in each other, demonstrating their involvement in the common cause and their readiness to meet long-term commitments.
  • Openness. All contacts between the partners are open, and they readily share the information needed for the joint business to succeed.
  • Integration. The partners build broad links between people at many organisational levels to smooth out the contradictions that arise in the course of work.
  • Formal status of the relationship. Each side’s responsibilities and the decision-making process are strictly defined.
  • Honesty. Cooperation is based on mutual trust, and the partners never act against each other’s views.

The trip to Hong Kong

We went to Hong Kong, met representatives of one of the largest jewellery companies there, came to an agreement and placed an order. It all sounds utterly ordinary, but for us it was the first time, and so exciting!

You have to understand that this was a time when nobody knew how any of this was done — was it even possible to bring such goods through customs? Today it is all clear, but back then a trip like that was like a spacewalk, with no idea of what would happen next. Not only did we make contacts, we also managed to bring the order back, going through the red channel at the airport on arrival. I think customs itself did not yet know how to handle such situations… In short, everything we had planned was done.

Diamonds, India and an Indian wedding

Diamonds were a product group we wanted to add to our business. Before that we had done well with imported silver — we connected more than two hundred foreign manufacturers. That line brought the company a noticeable rise in revenue, despite the difficulties with logistics: tagging, delivery, customs clearance. Imported silver is a broad range of fashion goods whose assortment changes constantly. We had to increase our warehouse space tenfold, along with staff and costs; it took a lot of time and changed many of the company’s business processes. But that is another story.

I very much wanted to find Russian diamond producers. We had never had this line before — only SUNLIGHT with imported fast-moving goods. A new product group required new contacts and suppliers. I asked the purchasing department to introduce me to the market leaders. Very quickly several companies came into view, one of them the largest in the world: a company from India working in Russia. Its Indian managers were very energetic people who could give many Russian entrepreneurs a head start. They visited our stores regularly, studied the business and looked at our assortment. I went round the stores twice a week and kept running into them. They did not work by the textbook; they seemed to be charged with energy for the job. We talked, agreed on a few things, and an invitation to a wedding arrived at once: their boss’s daughter was getting married. It meant going to India, to Jaipur. I didn’t think long: a wedding in India — how original, I must go. We travelled to a trade fair in Jaipur along with several other Russian entrepreneurs. We were put up in a hotel that had been bought out entirely for the wedding guests.

That trip astonished me; I had not expected a spectacle on such a scale and of such unheard-of generosity. The scope of the event exceeded all my expectations. The wedding lasted three days, with about three thousand guests from all over the world: representatives of big American online stores, suppliers, manufacturers. Each day the celebrations took place in a different setting, including UNESCO heritage sites. The sites were closed to visitors and all kinds of equipment was brought in. In the evenings — boats, music, dancing, several specially prepared halls and venues. In the daytime — entertainment, elephants, excursions. The wedding ceremony itself took place on the third day, and it was a day without alcohol. I grew up in the Caucasus and was always amazed by the scale and generosity of Eastern weddings, but they were nothing compared with the celebration I attended there.

We began working with this Indian company. The Indians are very organised, never let us down, and were always ready to develop and negotiate. There is much to learn from them: their special energy, activity and hard work.

Visiting diamond production in Vladivostok

Our Indian partners invited me to their diamond-cutting facility in Moscow, but I never managed to go, although I was curious to see the process. I did visit another facility they had opened a year earlier in Vladivostok — I was there practically at the opening. The three-storey plant was built in the centre of Vladivostok. It is high-tech production: the equipment alone is worth several million dollars. Large sums were invested in machines for sawing and cutting diamonds and in automating every process. Today India leads the world in cutting gemstones, diamonds included. That is not easy, and it requires serious investment.

How the Indian diamond business works

De Beers and ALROSA have some share in it, but I never looked into that. They even bring some goods in through Armenia. What surprised me was how well the managers are trained in banking products. By and large theirs is not a diamond business but a financial one, so the staff are trained in financial schemes. I think the Indian diamond business is so successful and growing so fast because they work like financiers and offer retail chains the best possible terms, allowing customers deferred payment for up to a year. Using hedging, managers can operate large sums and work with big companies. Even junior staff know the banks — VTB, Sberbank — and can introduce you to the heads of any department. If someone cannot get to grips with a product, they will explain it in detail and hand it to you on a plate. That, I think, is what impresses me most about them.

Summary and exercises

Key points

  • Reliable partners — suppliers, cutters, manufacturers — are as much a company asset as its stores and brand.
  • Personal trips to partners (Hong Kong, India, production in Vladivostok) give market knowledge that no report contains.
  • The Indian diamond business rests on families, trust and long relationships; respect for the partner’s culture is part of the deal.
  • A partnership takes years to build and one broken promise to destroy.
