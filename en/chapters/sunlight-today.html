What SUNLIGHT teaches today: modern marketing technologies

The previous chapters tell how the jewellery business was built: the leader, the people, the partners, the store, the window, advertising. This chapter is about how SUNLIGHT works today and why it is ahead. There is no single clever trick behind it. SUNLIGHT has put together an app, stores, delivery, financial services, its own media and production, and each part strengthens the others. The figures in this chapter come from public sources, listed at the end.

The app is the main shop window

A jewellery firm’s main shop window used to be a counter on a good street. Today it is a phone screen. According to SUNLIGHT’s management, more than 90% of the company’s online sales go through its mobile app. In chapter 4 Sergey Gribnyakov mentions about eight million downloads.

That is why the logo changed: the SUNLIGHT mark became an app icon (see chapter 9). It had to be rebuilt for a small square on a screen, because that is where customers now meet the brand most often.

The app offers what no window can: a catalogue of tens of thousands of items (the company speaks of nearly 30,000 unique pieces, not counting sizes), favourites, reminders, reservations for trying on in store, personal bonuses, a magazine and video. Above all, it gives direct contact with the customer and knowledge about them.

Lesson. If customers keep your app on their phones, you can reach them at any moment without intermediaries and without paying for every ad impression. It is the cheapest and most valuable channel.

Store and online as one system

SUNLIGHT runs an omnichannel model. According to the company, the chain has about a thousand stores across Russia; its App Store description lists more than 900 stores in more than 100 cities. In this system the stores do not compete with the Internet; they serve it:

  • they are pick-up points and fitting rooms: a piece can be reserved in the app and tried on in person;
  • they are a distributed warehouse: if a size does not fit, in large cities the right one is brought from another store in about an hour;
  • they are places of service and trust, where people come for advice, warranty claims and exchanges.

This reduces returns, which are very high when jewellery is bought “blind”.

Lesson. Do not set the store against the website. Customers do not separate them; to them it is one brand. The winner lets a purchase start in one channel and finish in another.

Fast and secure delivery

Jewellery purchases are often impulsive: a gift for tonight, a wish to treat yourself right now. So speed is a marketing tool. In November 2023 SUNLIGHT and Yandex Delivery launched express delivery of website and app orders in 116 cities. An order arrives in about an hour and a half on average, and delivery is free for orders from 4,990 roubles. There are other options too: pick-up in store, partner pick-up points, CDEK and Russian Post.

A detail worth remembering: SUNLIGHT couriers travel without logos, and shipments are insured. A branded van saying “jewellery” is an invitation to a robber. Here security in the jewellery trade limits the usual branding techniques.

Lesson. Shorten the path from desire to purchase. Every extra hour of waiting is a chance for the customer to change their mind.

Your own channel or marketplaces

Many jewellery brands sell actively through Wildberries and Ozon. SOKOLOV, for example, reported in 2024 that marketplaces brought it 13% of turnover. SUNLIGHT chose another path: the company says it is not on marketplaces, and that this is a matter of principle. Its arguments are not to hand the customer to a third-party platform, not to lose contact with them, not to dilute the brand, and not to depend on commissions the platform can raise at any time.

This decision has a price: marketplaces bring a huge flow of buyers that your own channel must attract by itself. Only a company with a strong brand, a large chain and a popular app can afford such a strategy.

Lesson. There is no universal answer. For a small brand a marketplace can be a launch pad. For a large chain, its own channel gives control over price, data and the customer relationship. The point is to decide deliberately and to calculate what a customer costs in each channel.

Price, bonuses and instalments

The company observes that price is the main factor in buying jewellery, followed by quality. Hence a set of mechanics:

  • bonus points that can be spent on purchases;
  • a birthday gift;
  • popular “two for the price of one” offers and big sales (for example, on 11.11);
  • pay-in-parts services (Yandex Split and T-Bank’s Dolyami) and bank instalment plans in stores. According to the company, roughly one customer in three or four is interested in instalments.

Different segments use instalments differently. Buyers under 35 treat them as a fashionable way to pay and often buy on impulse. Those over 35 use them to buy expensive items and see gold and diamonds as an investment.

Lesson. Promotional mechanics work, but they teach customers to wait for discounts. A strong chain combines promotions with reasons to buy without them: service, speed, choice and emotion.

Trust in the digital age

Buying gold and diamonds from a picture is a serious step. SUNLIGHT answers this in several ways. All items carry a state hallmark and are traced in Russia’s state system for precious metals and stones (GIIS DMDK). The warranty is up to two years, and a claim can be filed directly in the app, sometimes with just a photo. The Academy of Diamonds also has a Scientific Gemmological Centre (see Science), so professional expertise stands behind the quality of the stones.

Lesson. Online, trust is built not on words but on verifiable facts: hallmark, certificate, traceability, a clear warranty and easy returns.

The brand as media

SUNLIGHT does not just buy advertising in other people’s media; it has become a publisher itself. The holding includes the women’s magazine SUN MAG (readable inside the app), the men’s outlet DICH’, the industry portal “Jewellery Business” and the WIKI SUNLIGHT encyclopaedia (see Media). According to the company, its Telegram channel has more than 300,000 subscribers.

Another technique is research that becomes news. The company publishes its own analytics — for example, a survey of more than 3,000 Russian women on what an ordinary evening out costs, or sales results for 8 March. Journalists readily pick up such data, and the brand gains mentions without direct advertising.

Sonic branding

SUNLIGHT anthem “A Dream of a Diamond”

An own song is a rare brand tool in retail. Music is remembered faster than a logo and creates an emotion people then recognise in ads, in the store and in the app.

Lesson. Content holds attention between purchases. Jewellery is bought a few times a year, but people read and watch every day.

Video, community and bold experiments

SUNLIGHT Play is a video platform inside the app. According to CNews, it passed one million views in early 2023, and about 80% of its audience were young women. On top of it the company announced a dating service, which the press described as a combination of TikTok and Tinder: a short video in the profile, swipes, chatting through video “circles” without revealing a phone number, prizes for activity. Video chats for customers were also tested.

For a jewellery chain this is an unusual move. The logic: time spent in the app increases contact with the brand, and jewellery is a natural gift in a relationship.

Lesson. A leader can afford experiments that others find strange. But every experiment must be checked against numbers: does it bring purchases, or does it just drain resources and blur the brand?

The full cycle: pawnshop, buy-back, trade-in

The SUNLIGHT pawnshop network, buy-back and trade-in close the loop. A customer can bring in old gold, get cash or put it towards a new piece. For marketing this means one more reason to come to the store. The company also explains the difference between a pawnshop, buy-back and trade-in in plain words in its publications, which is another form of building trust.

Lesson. Think about the whole life of a piece of jewellery: purchase, wear, repair, exchange, sale. Each stage is a reason for contact with the brand.

A brand portfolio and its own production

One brand cannot speak to everyone. So the holding runs different marks for different buyers. SUNLIGHT is the mass-market “jewellery hypermarket”; Academy of Diamonds covers diamonds and a higher segment; Moscow Chain Factory is its own production of chains and bracelets; SERGEY GRIBNYAKOV watches are the founder’s personal image brand (see Holding). Own production gives control over price and assortment, and several brands reach different segments without diluting the main one.

Lesson. Grow not only wider (more stores) but deeper: your own production and your own marks for different segments.

Why SUNLIGHT is ahead

  1. An early bet on the app. The brand, the logo and sales were rebuilt around it.
  2. Omnichannel. Stores work for online, and online works for the stores.
  3. Control of the customer relationship. Its own channel instead of marketplaces, its own data and a direct line to the customer.
  4. Speed. Express delivery and moving stock between stores within an hour.
  5. Affordability. Wide choice, promotions, bonuses and pay-in-parts.
  6. Trust. Hallmarks, GIIS DMDK, warranty in the app and scientific gemmological expertise.
  7. An ecosystem. Media, video, pawnshops, production and different brands linked into one system.
  8. Readiness to change. The company’s history, from 1990s vouchers to a video platform, is a history of constant reinvention.

What remains a challenge

An honest textbook also names the model’s weak points, which anyone copying it should remember:

  • Dependence on discounts. Aggressive promotions teach customers to wait for sales and squeeze margins.
  • Risk of blurring the brand. The more services surround the jewellery (dating, video, media), the harder it is to say what the brand stands for.
  • Dependence on platforms. The app lives in app stores whose rules change regardless of the company’s wishes.
  • The cost of your own channel. Without marketplaces, every new customer has to be attracted on your own.

Summary and exercises

Key points

  • The main shop window of a modern jewellery chain is the app; stores become places for try-on, pick-up and service.
  • An own sales channel gives contact with the customer and data about them; staying off marketplaces is a deliberate strategy, not dogma.
  • Speed, instalments and bonuses turn an impulse into a purchase; hallmarks, traceability and warranty provide trust.
  • The brand becomes media and a community: magazines, video, research and Telegram hold attention between purchases.
