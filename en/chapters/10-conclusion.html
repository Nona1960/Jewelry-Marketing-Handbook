Chapter 12

In lieu of a conclusion

To strengthen and preserve your self-respect and to succeed in the jewellery market, you cannot do without charisma. The word means a kind of aura of a person who has developed their personality. Charisma is the ability to attract attention, hold it and, ultimately, succeed.

Charisma does not depend on age, gender or social position. It is an innate quality: you either have it or you don’t, and it can only be developed. It shows naturally, through a person’s behaviour and bearing. A narrow-minded person who is afraid to be themselves has no charisma. A person full of negative energy, with wrong ideas about the world, has little chance of showing their true self and proving their charisma. So be natural.

When the false Grand Duchess Anastasia appeared before Paris society in 1926, Prince Yusupov quickly saw through the deception. He wrote in his memoirs that she played her part badly: neither her face, nor her manners, nor her bearing resembled a grand duchess, and above all she lacked the innate simplicity and naturalness — the most charming quality of all the Romanovs, which nothing can destroy. Sometimes it takes practice to learn to behave naturally. To begin with, try asking yourself the following questions and answering them honestly.

  1. Your image. How do you look?
  2. Your public speaking. Can you express your thoughts briefly and clearly?
  3. Your ability to communicate. Can you build warm, trusting relationships?
  4. Your energy. How much positive energy and self-belief do you have?
  5. Your product knowledge. Do you have a deep knowledge of jewellery?
  6. Your sense of humour. Can you make people laugh?
  7. Your generosity of spirit. Do you prefer to take or to give warmth?
  8. Your sincerity and naturalness. Are you a sincere person? Do you wear masks?
  9. Your creativity (the ability to make an unforgettable impression). Are you like everyone else, or do you have your own individuality?
  10. Your reputation. What is your reputation?
  11. Your character (sociable, reserved, withdrawn).

Always strive to achieve more than you have, and lead your company with you.

And once you have got there, bring your company there too. In business a great deal depends on your attitude to competition, your habits around risk, and your confidence in the strategy and tactics of your affairs. Do not be afraid to live and create in an atmosphere of total uncertainty if you firmly know you are going your own way. For peace of mind it is very important to understand the game society around you is playing, in good time, and to slip out of that game in order to become free.

Freedom means understanding this game and, when necessary, resisting it.

Life, practice, positive and negative experience lead people to formulate certain rules that help them work successfully, avoid mistakes or correct those already made with the least losses. These rules may be more or less general, applying to life as a whole or to a particular area of relationships.

The authors take the liberty of offering the reader a few generalisations that may be useful in life and work as a whole, and therefore in achieving success in the jewellery market.

  1. There are always concrete reasons for our behaviour, so our ways of behaving — except those deliberately chosen to make others suffer — are neither right nor wrong.
  2. Almost all our behaviour is driven by our hopes for change for the better and by the wish to protect ourselves from what frightens us.
  3. Very often our behaviour towards other people is an attempt to manipulate their feelings and actions.
  4. People are good at heart. Negative behaviour is not innate; it is often linked to defensive reactions and insecurity.
  5. We are completely free in our own choices within the law, our physical abilities and our awareness of other options.
  6. True strength lies in being ready, while expressing our natural feelings, to deal with any negative consequences.
  7. Feeling regret for an act that hurt another person and considering yourself guilty of it are different things. You can feel regret without feeling guilty. Guilt means that, from our own point of view, we did something wrong.
  8. If self-protection becomes priority number one, caring for others becomes secondary.
  9. Our actions, even when they hurt other people’s feelings, are first of all actions for ourselves rather than against others. Likewise, when our partner defends themselves, they do it for themselves and do not consciously direct their actions against us.
  10. We are all inclined to judge; most often our judgement is the result of our conscious or unconscious fears.
  11. As a rule we are all very sensitive people and feel emotional discomfort when we lose the support of those close to us.
  12. Our partner’s approval matters greatly to us; therefore they have power over us, and vice versa.
  13. Our attempts to make another person fulfil our wishes at any cost lead to many negative consequences.
  14. By blaming others for our suffering, we miss the chance to learn more about ourselves.
  15. Seeking security and stability but unwilling to open up and become vulnerable, we often believe that others are responsible for our actions.
  16. We have power only over ourselves. We can control another person only as long as they want to be under our control.
  17. If we sincerely try to explore the reasons for another person’s anger and they shut themselves off from us, we are powerless to change their intention.
  18. Our unwillingness to suffer blocks a sober perception of reality.
