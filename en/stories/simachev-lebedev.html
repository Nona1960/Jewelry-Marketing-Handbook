Denis Simachev and Artemy Lebedev: an interesting comparison

In this chapter

  1. The “SUNLIGHT store closing” campaign
  2. How Artemy Lebedev made a website for SUNLIGHT
  3. Denis Simachev’s creativity

I don’t know either of them closely, only through their work, which you can see online. Simachev I know a little better.

I don’t think you should compare them by their clothes: Simachev is a fashion designer himself, so of course he’s a stylish guy, while Lebedev is simply a programmer. One is an artist, the other a programmer. The first wins by talent, the second by diligence and daily work.

Just look at how they present themselves online. Lebedev today has a YouTube channel — yes, he runs it professionally, but it is a fight for notification bells and likes, the least effort for the least work. With Simachev, just open his Instagram and everything becomes clear: every post has humour and some depth — in short, it is far more interesting to watch. I don’t know about others: there it’s likes and bells, here it’s real talent.

The most interesting thing about Lebedev is his price list and his slogan “Long. Expensive. F*cking great.” (that is his advertising line). His office is like any star designer’s office, built to justify steep prices — homely in style, but plainly pretentious.

I can compare Denis Simachev and Artemy Lebedev. With Lebedev we paid, say, two hundred thousand and in the end got nothing except that very “long, expensive, f*cking great”, and went on working with our own ideas and resources. With Simachev it was the opposite: our conversations were productive, we discussed various aspects of the business, sales in particular.

Then there are deadlines. More than once Denis Simachev sewed workwear for our staff at his Moscow factory — ten thousand items in total — in an unbelievably short time, a week or just three to five days. His people worked in three shifts because he had promised to deliver the order by a certain date. Lebedev, meanwhile, did it long, expensive and “great” — three months — and in the end none of it was any use to us. He took so long over the website that my programmers, designers and I solved the business problem ourselves.

The “SUNLIGHT store closing” campaign

The idea of announcing store closures came from Denis Simachev.

I remember we raised the subject of discounts: is it really only by constantly promising 70 and 80 per cent off in mailings that you can sell, or nobody will come to the store? What to do? And we had no imagination left to invent anything new. Denis said: announce… that the store is closing. I was puzzled: what, deceive people? He said: the word “sale” already irritates everyone, but a store closing is normal. People are curious about such events; they expect that a jeweller closing his shop will sell jewellery almost at cost or even at a loss, so they will happily open their piggy banks and hurry to invest them profitably in eternal values — gold and diamonds.

Denis added: write in the news that things are not going well for you; do nothing in the store — on the contrary, scatter boxes around, create an appearance of chaos. Drop the merchandising — no expensive posters or stickers. And most importantly, don’t worry, nothing bad will happen afterwards: people who bought today will come to the campaign again tomorrow. Just don’t run it every day — but once a year you certainly can.

I was surprised then: no, this is some kind of futuristic story! Writing about store closures might look normal now, but at a moment when you are just giving birth to your brand? You are thinking about how to raise sales, how to increase the value of the business, how to explain to people that your goods are good, how to prove that SUNLIGHT is not a synonym for dubious quality… And here someone suggests making things even worse, tightening the screws completely.

I put the idea to my staff. Naturally, at first they turned away from it, as I had. Then we decided: let’s try it in one city and send out a mailing. We tried, and the result exceeded expectations. Sales grew two- or threefold! The sales assistants couldn’t cope, and we had to reduce the number and frequency of SMS messages. The first “Store closing” campaign lasted about a month. We didn’t send the messages to everyone, simply because even extended double shifts couldn’t handle the flood of customers. In fact, prices did not fall much during the campaign, although on many items we did give a genuinely bigger discount.

In reality perhaps ten of our stores closed, but in the end we managed to build a national chain, which we are very proud of. So a simple conversation that cost us nothing at all let us earn back three times everything we had spent on Lebedev. That is Simachev’s talent! Lebedev knows how to earn well and to convince others that his services are justifiably expensive. Simachev is truly talented, and not narrowly specialised — in every field, you see? We ran this campaign for more than one year, and each time the return was better.

How Artemy Lebedev made a website for SUNLIGHT

About Lebedev. He has long been a media figure, and I have followed his website for a long time: a very well-organised, systematic site that still hasn’t changed. Artemy publishes everything, writes about everything, publishes books, sells fonts. You were still a toddler when he was already doing big website deals. A large team, a large volume of work, a long history — of course he holds the leading, hundred-per-cent-best place in websites and apps.

His slogan “Long. Expensive. F*cking great.” immediately sets the client up for a certain price level, but of course I wanted to bargain. He was already so “fat” that you could feel he wouldn’t haggle over trifles. As soon as I started negotiating I realised it wouldn’t be easy to get my way. He has had plenty of practice at negotiations and knows his worth. He conducts negotiations personally, bargains over prices, agrees with the client and takes a prepayment — on the toughest contract, with a prepayment of one to two hundred thousand euros — and yet, as you see, the website was of no use to me. I didn’t get what I wanted.

I copied another website, which in my opinion turned out better. It cost little; Artemy just adjusted the logo a bit and the online store’s workings.

I can’t say anything bad about Lebedev; he really knows his field. And the fact that he asks for a lot of money naturally shows that demand for him is high. He and his team are worth that money. I believe the websites Lebedev builds should cost that much, because practice has shown that they bear fruit if the business owner who hired him then promoted the site.

I have now come back to him as a client: we have signed trial contracts again, and my marketing team is working with Lebedev once more. There is no alternative: the programmers I have can’t produce anything decent without Lebedev.

As for small improvements — conversion: if something converts half a per cent better (product cards, for example), then at our volumes that work pays for itself in a week, or in two days. Does it make sense to be stingy over some fifty thousand dollars if it pays for itself within a month at most?

Denis Simachev’s creativity

He can certainly come up with something original. For example, he asks me: “Sergey, you have old dental crowns and bridges that you collect in the pawnshops, don’t you?” I say: well, of course. He says: “A pile like that, a big one, several kilos — no problem?” I say: no problem. He says: “There’s a contemporary art show coming up in America, and I want to transform that pile. Let’s do it together: your metal, my inspiration and creativity, and I have a great sculptor! We photograph the pile of dental gold, then melt the metal down and make a life-size portrait of the president (I have a real master for that!) and take it to the show in the USA under the Denis Simachev brand. And we build steps, say three, so that you have to climb up to our golden mask hanging on a red background — fabric, say — so it hangs higher than the other exhibits. In front of it, a label saying what it is made of and how (by the way, maybe the mask could have eyes of fine diamond chips?), so that people who want to see the portrait have to climb the steps. At the show it would attract a lot of attention, more than all the other pictures.”

An interesting idea, but it never happened: we had other things on our hands…

A few more thoughts about Denis…

However many wedding and party organisers I have looked at, if you want something truly cool there is no point going to anyone other than Simachev. He can make any event original, fashionable and anything but banal, so that everyone is impressed. No bored friends of the groom at the stag party, no glum faces at the wedding — he is a different kind of person from event-agency staff with their clichés. He can genuinely surprise and impress!
