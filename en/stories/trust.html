Trust and the art of communication

Sergey Gribnyakov attaches great importance to trust in dealing with colleagues and partners:

– A boss must inspire trust in their staff — some experts call this quality the emotional glue that binds a leader and the team. The compatibility of the head and the staff should be visible, and it is something senior management can be proud of. Do you agree?

– That’s a problem area for me. These are inner qualities, individual traits: some people have well-developed emotional intelligence, others less so. Mine is probably not the best, so there are shortcomings.

– Let’s discuss “dealing with people”. What do etiquette guides advise? If you want to make a good impression, behave naturally in any situation. Avoid extremes. Don’t be too tense or stiff — people won’t think well of you. Don’t be too serious or put on the air of a very clever, very busy person. Show interest in other people, their affairs and their problems. Be restrained and tactful. Express sincere approval. Notice everything you like in another person and tell them so. Pay more compliments. Listen attentively. Watch the other person’s body language. Try to find something you have in common, never look for differences. Show only positive signs of attention, such as praise, gratitude, an appreciative look. Avoid ridicule. To be considered decent and well-mannered, you will have to rid yourself of excessive curiosity, a quick temper, touchiness and vanity.

– The ABC of good manners, no doubt.

– Is there anyone around you who struck you as a maestro of communication?

– I’ve already mentioned the Chinese, of course. Entrepreneurs there can be of a very high calibre. Those are exactly my business partners. Now, admittedly, they no longer work in the jewellery market, or they do but not on the same scale. They’re older now, retired… Let me tell you more about one of them. He certainly made a dazzling impression: tall, with a wonderful way of talking…

– And how did he receive you?

– How? The best restaurants, the best views, wonderful company! For example: he invites you to a restaurant overlooking the arena in Basel. He lays on a table, cracks jokes. He calls over the young owner of the jewellery show from Las Vegas, who tells stories about working at that show. Meanwhile he’s pouring thirty-year-old Macallan under the table, brings out cigars, and it all turns into a joyful celebration of life. That’s how he received you — no stiffness, no overspending. He always made it both fun and warm-hearted.

– Did he shower you with praise?

– Oh no, people there aren’t that hypocritical — why compliments? His very manner was engaging, open and aimed at making sure the other person got the most pleasure out of the encounter. Sincere, without any affectation.

– Was there anyone unpleasant in your life, however influential?

– No one like that. Since my business dealings are with major manufacturers, they have all passed the market’s selection; the unworthy and dishonest probably died out in the first qualifying round. I have only met, and still meet, pleasant people.

– The extract.

– The quintessence.
